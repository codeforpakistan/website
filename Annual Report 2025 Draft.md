Departments gradually assumed full ownership of deployed systems, ensuring sustained adoption and long-term impact. ## E-Abyana: Transforming Irrigation Governance

**![E-Abyana Launch](/ar_2025/E Abyana Launch.jpg)**

For decades, irrigation water billing in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa relied on fragmented, manual records that were prone to errors, delays, and inconsistencies. Farmers often faced unclear or inaccurate bills, limited visibility into their payment history, and long administrative processes to resolve disputes, which contributed to revenue leakages for the government and mistrust among water users.

To address these challenges, the E-Abyana Digital Water Billing Management System was developed under the 8th Cycle of the KP Government Innovation Fellowship, a partnership between the Khyber Pakhtunkhwa Information Technology Board (KPITB) and Code for Pakistan. 

Officially launched by the Chief Minister of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa on 31 December 2025, E-Abyana digitises the entire irrigation billing process by mapping and digitising irrigation outlets and water channels, maintaining centralised records of farmers, landholdings, and crop data, generating secure electronic Abyana bills, and tracking payments in real time through interactive dashboards, while enhancing transparency, accuracy, and departmental oversight. This system makes irrigation billing simpler, faster, and more reliable for farmers, who now receive accurate, timely, and easily accessible bills, reducing disputes and administrative delays. At the same time, the centralised data and real-time monitoring support fair treatment and better-maintained irrigation infrastructure, resulting in more predictable water supply, improved trust in governance, and ultimately stronger agricultural productivity and livelihoods.

*“The launch of the E-Abyana Digital Billing System marks an important step towards transparent and efficient governance in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. By digitising core irrigation and billing processes, the government is making public services more accessible, accountable, and citizen-friendly. Digital transformation across provincial departments remains a key priority, with a strong commitment to completing end-to-end digitisation to improve service delivery for farmers and citizens alike.”*   
\- **Chief Minister Khyber Pakhtunkhwa**

The launch demonstrates how civic tech talent, when embedded within government systems, can facilitate long-term institutional reform rather than relying on standalone digital solutions. [Read more about E Abyana](https://codeforpakistan.org/stories/e-abyana-story).

## Fellowship Projects

In addition to E-Abyana, Code for Pakistan supported the deployment and adoption of several digital governance tools under the 8th cohort of the KP Government Innovation Fellowship Program: 

* [**NGOs Registration System**:](http://ngonpo.kpitb.online/login) The Social Welfare Department previously relied on slow, paper-based processes for NGO registration and compliance tracking, resulting in delays, bottlenecks, and limited transparency. The NGOs Registration System digitises registration, verification, and reporting workflows, enabling the department to monitor compliance in real time while making key information publicly accessible.   
  Deployed on departmental infrastructure and supported by structured training and clear documentation, the system streamlines NGO registration and oversight, reduces administrative backlogs, strengthens accountability, and helps build trust between civil society organisations and the government.  
* [**Advertisement Management MIS**:](https://www.codeforpakistan.org/node/319) Previously, manual reporting and payment workflows for government advertisements led to errors, delays, and weak financial oversight within the Information and Public Relations Department. The Advertisement Management MIS digitises and automates these processes, enabling staff to generate, track, and reconcile advertisement requests and payments through a single, integrated system.  
  With full departmental deployment and hands-on staff training, the system improves operational efficiency, strengthens transparency and financial accountability, and enables the department to deliver services more quickly while maintaining accurate, real-time resource tracking.  
* [**AI-Powered Plant Disease Detection App**](https://www.codeforpakistan.org/node/318): Farmers and agricultural extension officers previously faced delays in identifying crop diseases due to manual inspections and limited field expertise, resulting in ineffective interventions and crop losses. The AI-Powered Plant Disease Detection APP provides real-time, field-ready diagnostics along with actionable treatment recommendations.  
  Deployed on departmental infrastructure and supported with structured training, the system enables staff and farmers to respond quickly, protect crops, and increase yields, directly boosting agricultural productivity and improving livelihoods.  
* [**Government GPT**](https://kpgovtgpt.netlify.app/): Government departments have long struggled with fragmented internal knowledge, leading to staff spending excessive time searching for rules, procedures, and data across multiple systems, which slows service delivery. Government GPT provides AI-powered access to internal government knowledge, enabling staff to find accurate information quickly and make informed decisions.  
  With deployment on departmental systems and supported by training, the platform enhances efficiency, accelerates service delivery, and enables better governance across departments.

Each solution is accompanied by structured training and documentation, and aims to improve efficiency, transparency, and service delivery as it moves through testing and phased adoption.

# Civic Participation

## Guftugus: Engaging Citizens Across Cities

5 Civic Town Halls (Guftugus) were convened in 2025, each addressing locally relevant challenges and facilitating collaboration between citizens, government, and civil society.

* **Mardan:** The Guftugu in Mardan was hosted at the University of Engineering and Technology (UET) Mardan and convened 47 participants representing local government departments, civil society organisations, academia, media, youth groups, and climate practitioners. The dialogue focused on climate resilience and local governance, with participants highlighting challenges such as outdated water and sanitation infrastructure, limited fiscal and administrative authority of local governments, non-consultative land-use decisions, and the lack of localised climate data. Participants also identified opportunities to strengthen resilience through climate education, solar energy initiatives, and AI-enabled environmental monitoring. Through facilitated discussions and the “[What If Box](https://whatifbox.codeforpakistan.org/)” methodology, participants proposed practical interventions including improved drainage systems, expanded green spaces, and citizen-led awareness campaigns. As a key outcome, partnerships were initiated between the university, civil society actors, and local government stakeholders, establishing a foundation for future collaborative initiatives and follow-up engagement.  
  ![Guftugu Session - UET Mardan](/ar_2025/Mardan Guftugu.JPG)  
  Guftugu Session \- UET Mardan  
* **Charsadda:** Convened at Bacha Khan University Charsadda, the Guftugu brought together 46 participants from district government offices, civil society organisations, academia, entrepreneurs, students, and youth leaders. Discussions centred on infrastructure gaps, youth employability, education quality, energy shortages, and waste management. Participants emphasised the need for digital skills development, stronger academia, industry linkages, improved coordination between local government and educational institutions, and circular economy approaches for agriculture and waste. The dialogue resulted in the identification of ten local changemakers committed to sustaining civic engagement in Charsadda. A notable outcome was the emergence of a fortnightly Guftugu culture, where participants agreed to continue structured conversations on community priorities, supported by Code for Pakistan’s broader volunteer and civic engagement networks.  
  ![Guftugu Session - Bacha Khan University Charsadda](/ar_2025/Charsadda Guftugu.JPG)  
  Guftugu Session \- Bacha Khan University Charsadda  
* **Lahore:** The Lahore Guftugu convened 40 participants from the Punjab Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), universities, media personnel, civil society groups, private-sector representatives, and concerned citizens. Designed as an activity-based civic dialogue, the session focused on smog and air quality, moving beyond awareness toward collective problem-solving. Participants discussed key concerns, including vehicular and industrial emissions, brick kiln pollution, weak enforcement of environmental regulations, limited inter-departmental coordination, and the severe public health impacts of smog on children, elderly populations, and outdoor workers.  
  Importantly, the Guftugu enabled open and constructive dialogue between citizens and EPA representatives, allowing community experiences and institutional constraints to be discussed candidly. Through cross-sector breakout groups, participants co-developed a 90-Day Action Plan centred on student awareness, youth engagement, and early-stage citizen action on air pollution. The Guftugu also led to follow-up dialogues, alignment with the Chief Minister of Punjab’s Green Credit Program, and the formation of a cross-sector consortium to continue work on air quality initiatives.  
  ![Guftugu Session - Lahore](/ar_2025/Lahore Guftugu.jpg)  
  Guftugu Session \- Lahore  
* **Karachi:** Held in collaboration with the Climate Action Centre, the Karachi Guftugu brought together 30 participants, including representatives from government bodies, civil society organisations, research institutions, media, the private sector, and community leaders, particularly from underserved areas such as Machar Colony. The discussion focused on solid waste management, revealing that while technical solutions exist, progress remains constrained by fragmented governance, weak coordination among institutions, and limited incorporation of community knowledge.   
  Community participants emphasised the importance of behaviour change, localised solutions, and inclusive planning. A major outcome of the Guftugu was agreement on the Karachi Commitment Card, reflecting cross-sector willingness to contribute toward addressing waste management challenges. This led to the establishment of a **Citizen R\&D Cell**, a volunteer-driven platform designed to strengthen citizen government feedback loops, build local leadership from underserved and minority communities, and support inclusive policy implementation. Follow-up engagements with the Sindh Solid Waste Management Board explored solutions related to green energy, waste management systems, and environmental protection, while national media coverage, including a major mention in Dawn, amplified the initiative’s reach and credibility.  
  ![Guftugu Session - Karachi](/ar_2025/Karachi Guftugu.JPG)  
  Guftugu Session \- Karachi  
* **Faisalabad:** On 18 December, Code for Pakistan convened a Guftugu at the National Incubation Centre Faisalabad to examine the gender divide in agricultural technology and the systemic barriers limiting women farmers’ participation, leadership, and growth. Designed as an interactive, activity-based civic dialogue, the session brought together women farmers, government representatives, NGOs, legal experts, private sector and fintech actors, journalists, academics, and development practitioners. The discussion focused on how policy frameworks, financial systems, land ownership norms, data invisibility, and social constraints intersect to keep women confined to labour roles rather than leadership and ownership within emerging agri-tech ecosystems.  
  A key outcome of the Guftugu was the collective decision to establish a Faisalabad Leadership Circle, a farmer-centred collaboration framework intended to translate dialogue into coordinated action. The Circle brings together actors across the agricultural value chain, including agribusinesses, women farmer associations, civil society organisations, financial service providers, and policy influencers. As part of this effort, Fauji Fertiliser Company shared the launch of its agri tech platform and invited partners to actively connect women farmers and agripreneurs to ensure inclusive access. Capacity building and digital inclusion emerged as parallel priorities, with CIRCLE Women Association committing to digital literacy workshops and facilitating linkages with Digitt+, aligning community leadership, private sector platforms, and financial inclusion around a shared objective of strengthening women’s agency and economic participation in Faisalabad’s agri tech ecosystem.

# Civic Innovator

Student teams under the Civic Innovator Program developed **six projects** for government departments in 2025, delivering initial prototypes that were handed over to the respective departments for testing and refinement.

**Projects included:**

* [**On-Farm Water Management System**](https://www.codeforpakistan.org/node/320) **(Irrigation and Agriculture Department):** Provincial irrigation and agriculture departments lacked granular, on-farm data to inform water distribution and agricultural planning, contributing to inefficient water use.   
  A student team from CECOS University Peshawar, under the Code for Pakistan’s Civic Innovator Program, developed a prototype [On-Farm Water Management System](https://www.codeforpakistan.org/stories/pakistans-water-crisis-needs-data) to monitor farm-level water usage through integrated hardware and software components. The solution was prototyped and iteratively refined based on feedback from departmental stakeholders, with technical mentorship provided by CfP. The project aimed to strengthen evidence-based water management and promote more sustainable irrigation practices.   
  The same CECOS University team later received national recognition by winning a final year project competition organised by the Higher Education Commission.  
* [**Water and Sanitation Services Peshawar (WSSP)**](https://www.codeforpakistan.org/node/321)**:** Water and Sanitation Services Peshawar (WSSP) faced challenges due to limited real-time visibility into waste-bin status and waste collection operations, with monitoring processes that were largely manual and schedule-based. Students working under the Civic Innovator Program developed and tested an IoT-based Smart Waste Management prototype in consultation with WSSP officials, supported by meetings with department leadership and site visits.  
  With mentor-guided hardware and system development from CfP, the solution aimed to introduce IOT-sensor-based waste bin monitoring, enable timely alerts on bin fill levels, improve coordination between field teams and management, and support more data-informed waste collection planning within WSSP.  
* [**Outbreak Management System (Agriculture Department)**](https://www.codeforpakistan.org/node/322)**:** Agriculture department faced delays in detecting and responding to plant disease outbreaks due to the absence of centralized, real-time monitoring tools. In collaboration with the department of Agriculture, a student team developed an AI-powered Application for plant disease detection and outbreak identification.   
  Supported by CfP’s technical mentors, the solution aimed to strengthen early warning mechanisms, improve inter-departmental coordination, and support faster, data-driven disease detection for agricultural crops.  
* [**Vehicle Management System (Government Fleet Operations)**](https://www.codeforpakistan.org/node/323)**:** Multiple government departments struggled with inefficient management of official vehicle fleets, including limited visibility into vehicle allocation, usage, and maintenance. Students designed a digital Vehicle Management System to support fleet tracking, allocation workflows, and maintenance scheduling.   
  Developed in consultation with government users and supported by CfP mentors, the system aimed to improve transparency, reduce misuse of public assets, and optimise fleet utilisation across departments.  
* [**Supply Chain Management System**](https://www.codeforpakistan.org/node/324) **(Public Procurement and Inventory Management):** Procurement and inventory management across government departments were often manual and fragmented, resulting in delays, errors, and limited accountability. A student team developed a prototype Supply Chain Management System to digitise procurement workflows and inventory tracking.   
  Through structured mentorship and engagement with public-sector stakeholders, the solution aimed to introduce greater transparency, efficiency, and traceability into government supply chains.  
* [**Interactive Digital Map for Peshawar Zoo**](https://www.codeforpakistan.org/node/325) **(Department of Wildlife):** The Peshawar Zoo administration lacked digital tools to support visitor engagement and streamline internal operations. Students developed an interactive digital system in collaboration with zoo management to enhance visitor information, engagement, and administrative coordination. The solution aimed to improve the public experience at the zoo while supporting more efficient day-to-day management of the facility.

Collectively, these projects highlight the strength of multi-institutional collaboration between students, universities, government departments, and CfP. By engaging universities such as CECOS University Peshawar, public entities including WSSP, provincial health and irrigation departments, and the Peshawar Zoo, and national institutions like the Higher Education Commission, the Civic Innovator Program creates pathways for youth to engage with civic challenges early in their academic and professional journeys. This approach not only builds practical skills but also nurtures a generation of technologists and leaders committed to public service, civic responsibility, and collaborative governance.

## Our Volunteers: From Awareness to Action

Code for Pakistan’s volunteers are central to translating civic dialogue into tangible impact. Across 2025, youth and community engagement have taken multiple forms, from campaigns and civic labs to volunteer-driven mapping initiatives.

**\#SmogWarriors \- Citizen-led Air Quality Advocacy**

Following the Lahore Guftugu on air pollution, we aim to mobilise the volunteers as \#SmogWarriors, turning awareness into action. Each volunteer will become a(n):

* Observer: documenting smog conditions in their city  
* Communicator: informing the public about the causes and impacts of air pollution  
* Mobilizer: engaging peers, tagging authorities, and promoting collective responsibility

Through digital and offline activities, volunteers will share real-time Air Quality Index (AQI) updates, fact-based posts, polls, infographics, and health-based content. They will organise discussions on campus and in communities, building a youth-led network advocating for clean air and climate action.
